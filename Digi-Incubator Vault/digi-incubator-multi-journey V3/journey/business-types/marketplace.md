# Business Type — Marketplace

> Any startup that connects two or more sides — and earns from the transactions between them.

← [[journey-index|Journey Index]]

---

## Examples
Hyperlocal delivery platform, freelance talent marketplace, agri-trade platform connecting farmers to buyers, equipment rental marketplace, hospitality booking platform.

## The Metrics That Matter

| Metric | Definition | When it matters |
|---|---|---|
| GMV | Gross Merchandise Value — total transaction value on the platform | Stage 4 onwards |
| Take rate | % of GMV the platform keeps | Stage 3 planning |
| Platform revenue | GMV × take rate | Stage 4 onwards |
| Supply side — active listings / providers | How many sellers / service providers are active | Stage 4 |
| Demand side — active buyers | How many people are buying or requesting | Stage 4 |
| Liquidity rate | % of supply that actually transacts each month | Stage 5 |
| Supply CAC | Cost to acquire one active provider | Stage 3 |
| Demand CAC | Cost to acquire one active buyer | Stage 3 |
| Repeat transaction rate | % of users who transact more than once | Stage 5 |

## The Cold Start Problem

Every marketplace faces it: supply won't come without demand, and demand won't come without supply. The GTM Agent explicitly plans for this — usually by starting in one dense geography, building supply density, then unlocking demand.

## The Key Scaling Question

> *At what GMV does the platform become self-sustaining — where take rate revenue covers all operational costs without subsidy?*
